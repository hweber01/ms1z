Lesson 2.1: Goal, Task, Constraint

In this lesson we'll pin down three words we'll use in every lesson from now on: goal, task and constraint.

They sound simple. But most planning problems start when people mix them up.

A goal is the end state. It describes what has to be true when we're done.

Not what we do. What is true at the end.

A simple test: can you picture the moment it's done? Everyone who eats is full. You can picture that.

A task is an action. It usually starts with a verb: buy, cook, call, send.

A task is only worth doing if it moves you toward the goal.

And tasks can change. If the store is closed, buying groceries stops being a good task. But the goal is still there.

A constraint is a limit. Time, money, rules, things you can't use.

Lunch is at one o'clock. We have twenty dollars. One of the kids is allergic to peanuts.

Constraints don't tell you where to go. They tell you which roads are closed.

Why does this matter?

When a task is treated as a goal, people keep doing it even after it stops helping.

When a constraint is packed into the goal, every change in the limit feels like failure. Lunch at one becomes lunch at a quarter past one, and suddenly the whole plan feels broken. Even though everyone still gets fed.

Now let's try it at work. Your manager says: get the report to the client by Friday.

Which part is which? Send the report is a task. By Friday is a constraint.

So where's the goal? It isn't there. And that's very common.

The goal is what should be true after the client reads the report. Maybe the client approves the next phase. Maybe they have what they need to make a decision.

When the goal is missing, ask. One simple question works almost every time: what should be true when this is done?

Most managers are glad to answer it. And once you know the answer, you can do a much better job than just sending a file on time.

So, three words. A goal is the end state. A task is an action that moves you toward it. A constraint is a limit you work within.
