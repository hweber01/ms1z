Lesson 1.1: Busy Is Not Progress

You come in early. You leave late. Your team is busy all day. Every task on the list gets done.

And still, at the end of the month, your manager asks: why aren't we where we need to be?

If that sounds familiar, this course is for you.

Here is a problem that shows up again and again, in almost every team.

People are very good at doing tasks. Fewer people know where the task came from, or why it exists at all.

When you don't know why a task exists, you keep doing it even after it stops helping anyone.

So let's start with the most important idea in this course.

A goal is the end state. It is what has to be true when we're done.

A task is an action. It is one possible way to get there.

Goals stay. Tasks can change, be added or be dropped, as long as we still reach the goal.

Let's make this simple. It's nine in the morning, and lunch needs to happen.

Most people would say the goal is to make lunch. But that's a task.

The goal is: everyone who eats will be full.

Notice what changed. Once the goal is clear, cooking is just one option. You could heat leftovers. You could order in. If there's no food and no money, you could find a place that gives out free meals.

The task changed. The goal didn't.

Now take this to work. As a new manager, you live between two levels.

Above you, someone hands you tasks. Below you, your team waits for you to hand them tasks.

If you pass tasks down without the goal behind them, your team will do exactly what you said, even when the situation changes and it no longer makes sense.

If they know the goal, they can adapt on their own.

By the end of this course, you'll be able to take any assignment from your manager and turn it into a clear goal.

You'll see the full picture before you start planning.

You'll give your team tasks, and the goal behind them.

And when things change, and they will, you'll adapt without losing the goal.

The method behind all of this is called Goal-First Planning. It's built on principles military planners have used for a long time, adapted for everyday work.

You won't just watch. After most lessons there's a short exercise.

In the middle of the course you'll run a busy restaurant kitchen on a Saturday night. At the end, you'll organize a football tournament, with surprises along the way.

And finally, you'll run the whole method on a real challenge from your own team.
