Exercise 1: Task or Goal?

Part 1. Mark each sentence as a Goal (an end state: what has to be true when you're done) or a Task (an action: one way to get there).

1. Everyone on the team knows the new process.
2. Send the weekly report.
3. Customers who call get an answer the same day.
4. Book a meeting room for the training.
5. The new hire can work on their own.
6. Update the slides for the client meeting.
7. Every guest at the event is seated before it starts.
8. Call three suppliers for prices.
9. All the team's laptops work on Monday morning.
10. Hire a new designer.

Part 2: Reverse derivation
- Write down five items from your to-do list for this week.
- Next to each item, write the goal it serves.
- Mark the items with no clear goal. For each one, decide: drop it, change it, or ask your manager what it is for.

Answer key
1. Goal. It describes what is true at the end.
2. Task. An action. The goal behind it might be: the people who read it know where the project stands.
3. Goal. It describes what is true for every customer who calls.
4. Task. An action. The goal behind it might be: the team can use the new tool.
5. Goal. An end state you can picture.
6. Task. An action. The goal behind it might be: the client agrees to the next step.
7. Goal. It describes the moment when it is done.
8. Task. An action. The goal behind it might be: we buy at a price we can afford.
9. Goal. It describes what is true on Monday morning.
10. Task, even though it sounds big. Hiring is an action. The goal behind it might be: design work gets done on time. Sometimes there is another way to reach that goal.
