Lesson 2.2: Why “Mission” Confuses Everyone

There's one word we'll avoid in this course, and it's worth a few minutes to explain why. The word is mission.

In everyday business English, a mission sounds like a big purpose. A company's mission statement is about why it exists.

But in many workplaces, a mission is simply a job you were given to do. Your mission today is to finish the report.

In military planning, a mission usually holds both: what to do, and why, in one sentence.

That works when everyone has been trained to read it that way.

In most offices, people hear only the first half, the task. And the purpose gets lost.

Mission isn't the only slippery word. Objective, target, deliverable, even priority.

Each one can mean a goal, a task or a constraint, depending on who's talking.

So in this course we'll stick to three plain words: goal, task and constraint.

When you hear them, you'll know exactly what they mean.

And when someone at work hands you a mission, or an objective, or a deliverable, translate it.

Ask two questions. What do you need me to do? And what should be true when it's done?

The first answer is the task. The second is the goal.
